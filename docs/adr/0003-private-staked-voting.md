# Private staked voting on MagicBlock; security from slashing, not the weight curve

The intended final escalation—contingent on the feasibility gate below—is a private, per-dispute, USDC-staked vote with **linear weight** (1 originally staked USDC = 1 vote), settled by **Schelling-point slashing**: losing-side voters are slashed, winning-side voters are rewarded from the losing side. Devnet requires a dual Vote Quorum of at least 3,000 USDC in total Gross Voting Stake and at least 10 distinct voting wallet addresses. Both quorum and the 67% calculation use the original stake locked before the Voting Fee; fees are deducted only during settlement. Once quorum is met, `True`, `False`, or `Unresolvable` must reach an inclusive 67% to win. Checked integer cross-multiplication makes exactly 67.000% sufficient without division rounding. A failed quorum or no 67% winner produces the no-fault `NoConsensus` outcome ([ADR-0007](0007-unresolvable-vs-no-consensus.md)). One deployment-wide **Voting PER** serves every Devnet Vote Round. Actual USDC is held by eSPL's shared per-mint global vault on Solana; Vote Initialization credits all three Participant Bonds to a round-scoped delegated eATA/balance record that Opal calls the logical **Vote Settlement Vault** inside the PER. Before casting, a wallet must fund a reusable **Private Voting Balance** through MagicBlock's private-payment/eSPL flow; an accepted Vote atomically locks its stake into that same logical settlement balance. Votes are intended to remain private during the Voting Window. The Assertion cannot become `Resolved` on Solana until an authenticated aggregate projection is public there; bounded publication later exposes each wallet's position. We reject sub-linear/"quadratic" weighting, time-weighted voting (TWAV), and OPAL-weighting.

**Why linear + slashing.** No per-token weight curve can resist _both_ whales and Sybils — they are the same dial: sub-linear weight resists whales but _rewards_ token-splitting across identities, while linear weight is Sybil-neutral but whale-exposed. The real defense is economic — slashing makes being wrong cost money, which is Sybil-neutral and whale-deterring — so weight stays **linear**.

**Why dual quorum.** Without a stake quorum, a dust-sized position could determine all three Participant Bonds when turnout is low. Requiring 3,000 USDC makes the vote economically substantial before it can assign fault. Requiring 10 wallet addresses avoids treating one large position as broad participation, while remaining only a wallet-level diversity check: a single person can still control several addresses. Both conditions are required; neither substitutes for the other. Every wallet with a valid Vote counts equally toward the address condition, including each bonded wallet that participates; excluding bonded wallets would create a role-specific restriction that could be bypassed with another address.

**Why private.** A public running tally collapses the vote into a Keynesian beauty contest: under public voting + slashing, "follow the visible majority" is the safe, dominant strategy, so the oracle tracks the loudest mover rather than the truth. Sealing the tally removes the visible leader, forcing voters to coordinate on the only remaining focal point — the truth. TWAV was rejected because weighting earlier votes more _empowers_ a first-moving attacker and down-weights late honest correction — the inverse of its intent.

**Why pre-funded.** A voter who transfers their exact stake from a public Solana USDC account while casting a vote reveals their wallet and stake amount even when the selected outcome is hidden. Funding is therefore a separate action. The wallet deposits at least 1 USDC per deposit into a reusable Private Voting Balance before casting; the vote instruction locks stake from that private balance inside the PER. A wallet may vote in multiple Vote Rounds concurrently when its unlocked balance covers each new stake. The one-Vote limit applies independently per wallet per Vote Round, and every round maintains its own immutable position and lock. Only unlocked balance may fund another Vote or be withdrawn. Active voting stake cannot be withdrawn, and a finalized payout is not part of the balance until its owner claims it from the Vote Settlement Vault. A wallet may withdraw any positive amount up to its unlocked balance; Opal imposes no withdrawal minimum. Moving available funds back to the public Solana wallet is a separate, manual withdrawal. A base-layer deposit or withdrawal remains publicly observable and may permit timing or amount correlation, so pre-funding is an anti-linkage measure, not an anonymity guarantee.

**Product boundary.** MagicBlock's hosted Private Payments API may build wallet deposit and withdrawal operations. Vote casting and payout claims require a pinned onchain eSPL integration inside the Opal instruction—delegated canonical token accounts with SPL CPI or the direct `ephemeral-spl-api` surface, selected by the feasibility spike—so the token movement can be atomic with Opal's stake or claim marker. Opal—not MagicBlock—enforces one vote per wallet per Vote Round, the selected outcome, stake locking, the Voting Window, the 67% threshold, fees, slashing, and rewards. The private token balance, Opal vote state, and logical Vote Settlement Vault must be writable on the same router-resolved PER endpoint. Private vote records do not settle publicly before the window closes; terminal settlement freezes aggregate results, then bounded publication produces one permanent public position account per voter rather than a growable vector on the Vote Round. See [ADR-0017](0017-per-voter-public-position-accounts.md).

**Implementation feasibility gate.** Before Devnet can rely on this design, a focused PER/eSPL spike must prove that an arbitrary voter can authorize one immutable Vote and lock stake without receiving read access to the shared running tally or other wallets' positions. The documented MagicBlock permission model currently couples membership with read access, while excluding all voters may also prevent their writes; merely “keeping everything private until the end” does not prove the required write-without-read path. A shared member list must also support an unbounded voter set without requiring pre-funded permission space for a fixed maximum. The same spike must prove PDA-controlled eSPL bond custody; reusable voter-balance locking; direct zero-deposit eATA initialization for a bonded non-voter's claim destination; base-created receipt delegation and restoration; and the exact authorities required for setup retry, finalization, bounded publication, participant claims, and treasury delivery. It must also identify whether hosted MagicBlock provisions the validator-scoped eSPL mint, transfer queue, global vault, and rent-PDA infrastructure; if not, the Devnet deployment operator pays that one-time infrastructure cost outside protocol treasury and participant reserves. No operation is called permissionless until the spike proves that every private-state or eSPL authority it needs can be supplied by an Opal PDA rather than a user or operator signer. If the write-without-read primitive or required eSPL custody path is unavailable, the MVP must adopt a different construction—such as commit-reveal or independently sealed per-voter records with a separately trusted or attested aggregator—before claiming that a shared tally is private.

**Why co-locate all vote-stage collateral.** Participant Bonds begin in the public Solana Bond Vault, while voting stake begins in private eSPL balances. The unified Slashed Pool and Reward Weight rules mix both sources and pay both bonded participants and voters. Leaving them on separate accounting surfaces would require multi-step partial-settlement recovery. Instead, Vote Initialization deposits the full Bond Vault balance into eSPL's shared per-mint global vault and credits the round's delegated settlement-balance record. Each accepted Vote then atomically moves its stake into that same logical balance. The seven-day Voting Window starts only after the bond balance and private state are verified on the vote's PER endpoint. This is a protocol-triggered technical transition, not a separate vote-setup window.

**Why one Voting PER.** A reusable private eSPL balance is delegated to one ER validator, and one ER transaction can mutate only co-located accounts. Choosing validators per Vote Round would force balance migration or prevent the same balance from participating across concurrent rounds. Devnet therefore fixes one Voting PER validator identity at deployment for every Private Voting Balance, Vote Settlement Vault, and private vote record. Clients resolve its live FQDN through the router rather than hard-coding an endpoint.

**Retryable Vote Initialization.** Accepting the second dispute atomically locks the third bond, records an immutable setup nonce, mint, Voting PER validator, setup payer, and reserve accounting in `VoteDisputeAccount`, and transfers the configured capped lamport reserve from the Vote Disputer into a separate zero-data, System-owned `VoteSetupPayer` PDA; insufficient bond or reserve funding rejects the whole transaction. Opal can sign for that PDA only through `invoke_signed`, while its System ownership permits the System Program to debit it for fixed account creation and delegation rent. The payer never covers a later caller's transaction or provider fee. Subsequent phases persist monotonic receipts for bond-to-eSPL preparation, delegation, PER permission/session initialization, and readiness. An exact replay is a no-op and conflicting state is an error; no phase can transfer funds twice, change the validator, or replace the original refund recipient. The feasibility spike must prove that a PDA-signed low-level MagicBlock CPI can record `VoteSetupPayer` as the rent payer, making every technical phase callable by an arbitrary fee-paying relayer. This is a Devnet release gate, not an optional signer fallback: if it fails, the cost/liveness design and this ADR must change before launch. Cleanup returns eligible rent/deposits to the payer PDA, and a bounded reconciled refund forwards surplus to the immutable setup payer. If actual onchain costs exceed the cap, the Vote Disputer or an optional external integrator may top up the payer PDA; treasury funds, reimbursements, and retry rewards are unavailable.

The PER phase establishes the authoritative Voting Window and writes a pre-created, delegated `VoteReadyReceipt`; commit-and-undelegate restores that receipt to Solana, where a permissionless idempotent `publish_vote_ready` projection replaces the target semantics of today's `open_vote`. Votes may already be valid on the PER while Solana still reports `PendingVote`; clients deciding whether they can cast query authenticated PER state rather than treating the lagging base state as the acceptance gate. A delayed projection never creates or resets the window. Avoiding this temporary split would require another base-to-PER acknowledgment phase, which the minimal MVP rejects. The Devnet MVP defines no setup abort or timeout: a prolonged MagicBlock outage leaves the Assertion and bonds in `PendingVote` until setup resumes. This is an explicit liveness risk, not an assumption that cross-runtime work rolls back with the original dispute transaction.

**Voting deadline.** A Vote counts only when its transaction is accepted and recorded on the Voting PER while the seven-day Voting Window is still open (`execution_time < voting_deadline`). Signing or submitting a transaction before the deadline does not reserve a Vote; a transaction that lands after the deadline is rejected.

**Competing submissions.** The first accepted Vote transaction for a wallet-and-round pair creates the wallet's immutable position. Any retry or competing transaction that reaches the program afterward is rejected without changing the recorded outcome or stake and without locking additional USDC. A rejected or otherwise failed Vote transaction creates no position and incurs no Opal Voting Fee, although external network or provider execution costs may still apply. A client that receives an already-voted error must read the existing position rather than assume no Vote exists.

**Vote-stage payout boundary.** All fees, refunds, slashing, and pro-rata rewards are calculated from the logical Vote Settlement Vault balance. The PER finalizer freezes per-outcome stake and position counts, exact accumulated fees, the Slashed Pool, Reward Weight, and an `unclaimed_positive_position_count`; it does not claim to know the sum of every independently floored payout without enumerating wallets. Each successful positive claim decrements that count. Once it reaches zero, checked balance and claimed-total reconciliation classifies the remaining sub-position-count residual as treasury-owned pro-rata dust. All three vote-stage Bond Fees and the exact sum of per-position Voting Fees move to `TreasuryStagingBalance` at settlement; dust follows only after the last claim. Every eligible participant—including the asserter and either disputer—must claim their vote-stage payout into a Private Voting Balance once authoritative PER settlement freezes the round, which may precede the lagging Solana `Resolved` projection. Each voter creates, funds, and delegates its own canonical balance before casting; Opal provides no protocol-funded sponsorship. A bonded participant that does not vote need not have a balance during Vote Initialization or finalization: its positive entitlement still becomes `Claimable`, remains reserved without expiry, and can be claimed after the owner directly initializes and delegates the canonical empty eATA destination and pays its SOL setup costs. The feasibility spike must prove that initializer; receiving the payout must not require a positive USDC deposit. The claim validates but never creates or redirects that destination. Non-vote settlement paths continue paying public Solana token accounts automatically. A manual withdrawal is required to move a claimed private balance back to Solana. See [ADR-0009](0009-claim-based-vote-payouts.md).

**Intended MVP privacy boundary, feasibility-gated.** During the Voting Window, the Private Voting Balance, per-outcome totals, wallet choices, and stake amounts are intended to be permissioned/private. Public Solana still exposes deposits into and withdrawals from the private balance. PER settlement freezes the final outcome, whether the 67% threshold was reached, and aggregate stake per outcome. The Assertion must not become `Resolved` on Solana until an authenticated projection makes those aggregates public there. Wallet-level outcome, stake, exact payout, and `Claimable`, `Claimed`, or `Slashed` status become public only as bounded post-finalization publication creates each `VotePositionAccount`; the public mapping remains incomplete until `PositionPublicationComplete`. A wallet's total Private Voting Balance remains private. Permanent voter privacy is a possible post-MVP improvement, not an MVP guarantee.

**Considered options.** Public + anti-sniping (rejected: bandwagon-prone); on-chain commit-reveal (viable; rejected for MVP in favour of MagicBlock's amount-hiding and no-reveal UX); keeping bonds on Solana during voting (rejected: the unified pool would require multi-step cross-runtime payouts and partial-settlement recovery); quadratic + proof-of-personhood (`Vision`).

**Consequences.** MagicBlock is the MVP's critical-path dependency (private-balance authentication, delegation, router placement, PER availability, private token movement, and terminal settlement). Voters must create and fund their balance before voting; bonded non-voters may wait until they have a claim, then use the spike-proven direct initializer for an empty destination without first depositing USDC. Every recipient manually withdraws when they want public-wallet liquidity because vote settlement itself does not initiate a withdrawal. Opal charges no protocol fee on Private Voting Balance deposits or withdrawals and provides no protocol-funded sponsorship. The Vote Disputer funds the capped onchain rent/delegation setup reserves in addition to its Participant Bond; each later caller pays its own transaction/provider fees, eligible returned rent or delegation deposits flow through `VoteSetupPayer` to the immutable original setup payer, and permanent record rent and external charges are not reimbursed. Deposits and withdrawals return machine status `Pending`, `Completed`, or `RecoveryNeeded`; participant interfaces render the last value as “Recovery Needed.” Only destination-balance confirmation produces `Completed`, so source-transaction confirmation alone never changes usable balance. The balance, Vote positions, and payout claims remain bound to the original wallet. Opal has no admin recovery or destination override for a lost key; any recovery must come from the wallet's own external mechanism. Only that wallet may close its reusable Private Voting Balance, and only after its own amount and active locks are zero. Outstanding round claims survive closure and require recreating the same canonical empty destination before claiming. Raw claim state and the logical settlement balance remain delegated while any non-expiring payout is unclaimed, making the configured PER a post-finalization availability dependency; Devnet has no base-layer claim fallback or admin recovery. Participants receive the intended anti-bandwagon privacy only if the feasibility gate passes, and should expect their wallet, choice, and stake to be publicly attributable after bounded publication completes. The irreducible residual is a 51%-of-stake attack, mitigated by bond/reward sizing and voter-base growth; proof-of-personhood is the `Vision` hardening. Voter-reward sufficiency at small scale is an open economic question. See [ADR-0002](0002-trusted-llm-resolver.md) (the vote backstops the LLM) and [ADR-0004](0004-single-asset-usdc.md).
