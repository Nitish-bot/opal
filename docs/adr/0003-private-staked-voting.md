# Private staked voting on MagicBlock; security from slashing, not the weight curve

The final escalation is a private, per-dispute, USDC-staked vote with **linear weight** (1 originally staked USDC = 1 vote), settled by **Schelling-point slashing**: losing-side voters are slashed, winning-side voters are rewarded from the losing side. Devnet requires a dual Vote Quorum of at least 3,000 USDC in total Gross Voting Stake and at least 10 distinct voting wallet addresses. Both quorum and the 67% calculation use the original stake locked before the Voting Fee; fees are deducted only during settlement. Once quorum is met, `True`, `False`, or `Unresolvable` must reach an inclusive 67% to win. Checked integer cross-multiplication makes exactly 67.000% sufficient without division rounding. A failed quorum or no 67% winner produces the no-fault `NoConsensus` outcome ([ADR-0007](0007-unresolvable-vs-no-consensus.md)). One deployment-wide **Voting PER** serves every Devnet Vote Round. Vote Initialization moves/delegates all three Participant Bonds into a **Vote Settlement Vault** there. Before casting, a wallet must fund a reusable **Private Voting Balance** through MagicBlock's private-payment/eSPL flow; Opal locks voting stake from that balance into the same vault. Votes are kept private during the Voting Window; after settlement, aggregate totals and each wallet's selected outcome and stake become public. We reject sub-linear/"quadratic" weighting, time-weighted voting (TWAV), and OPAL-weighting.

**Why linear + slashing.** No per-token weight curve can resist _both_ whales and Sybils — they are the same dial: sub-linear weight resists whales but _rewards_ token-splitting across identities, while linear weight is Sybil-neutral but whale-exposed. The real defense is economic — slashing makes being wrong cost money, which is Sybil-neutral and whale-deterring — so weight stays **linear**.

**Why dual quorum.** Without a stake quorum, a dust-sized position could determine all three Participant Bonds when turnout is low. Requiring 3,000 USDC makes the vote economically substantial before it can assign fault. Requiring 10 wallet addresses avoids treating one large position as broad participation, while remaining only a wallet-level diversity check: a single person can still control several addresses. Both conditions are required; neither substitutes for the other. Every wallet with a valid Vote counts equally toward the address condition, including each bonded wallet that participates; excluding bonded wallets would create a role-specific restriction that could be bypassed with another address.

**Why private.** A public running tally collapses the vote into a Keynesian beauty contest: under public voting + slashing, "follow the visible majority" is the safe, dominant strategy, so the oracle tracks the loudest mover rather than the truth. Sealing the tally removes the visible leader, forcing voters to coordinate on the only remaining focal point — the truth. TWAV was rejected because weighting earlier votes more _empowers_ a first-moving attacker and down-weights late honest correction — the inverse of its intent.

**Why pre-funded.** A voter who transfers their exact stake from a public Solana USDC account while casting a vote reveals their wallet and stake amount even when the selected outcome is hidden. Funding is therefore a separate action. The wallet deposits at least 1 USDC per deposit into a reusable Private Voting Balance before casting; the vote instruction locks stake from that private balance inside the PER. A wallet may vote in multiple Vote Rounds concurrently when its unlocked balance covers each new stake. The one-Vote limit applies independently per wallet per Vote Round, and every round maintains its own immutable position and lock. Only unlocked balance may fund another Vote or be withdrawn. Active voting stake cannot be withdrawn, and a finalized payout is not part of the balance until its owner claims it from the Vote Settlement Vault. A wallet may withdraw any positive amount up to its unlocked balance; Opal imposes no withdrawal minimum. Moving available funds back to the public Solana wallet is a separate, manual withdrawal. A base-layer deposit or withdrawal remains publicly observable and may permit timing or amount correlation, so pre-funding is an anti-linkage measure, not an anonymity guarantee.

**Product boundary.** MagicBlock's private-payment/eSPL flow supplies deposit, private-balance, transfer, and withdrawal mechanics. Opal—not MagicBlock—enforces one vote per wallet per Vote Round, the selected outcome, stake locking, the Voting Window, the 67% threshold, fees, slashing, and rewards. The private token balance, Opal vote state, and Vote Settlement Vault must be writable on the same router-resolved PER endpoint. Private vote records do not settle publicly before the window closes; terminal settlement freezes aggregate results, then bounded publication produces one permanent public position account per voter rather than a growable vector on the Vote Round. See [ADR-0017](0017-per-voter-public-position-accounts.md).

**Why co-locate all vote-stage collateral.** Participant Bonds begin in the public Solana Bond Vault, while voting stake begins in private eSPL balances. The unified Slashed Pool and Reward Weight rules mix both sources and pay both bonded participants and voters. Leaving them on separate runtimes would require a multi-step cross-runtime payout with partial-settlement states. Instead, Vote Initialization moves/delegates the full Bond Vault balance into the Vote Settlement Vault and verifies that it shares the vote's PER endpoint. The seven-day Voting Window starts only after this succeeds. This is an automatic technical transition, not a separate vote-setup window.

**Why one Voting PER.** A reusable private eSPL balance is delegated to one ER validator, and one ER transaction can mutate only co-located accounts. Choosing validators per Vote Round would force balance migration or prevent the same balance from participating across concurrent rounds. Devnet therefore fixes one Voting PER validator identity at deployment for every Private Voting Balance, Vote Settlement Vault, and private vote record. Clients resolve its live FQDN through the router rather than hard-coding an endpoint. The Devnet protocol model assumes automatic Vote Initialization completes successfully and defines no participant-facing timeout or abort outcome.

**Voting deadline.** A Vote counts only when its transaction is accepted and recorded on the Voting PER while the seven-day Voting Window is still open (`execution_time < voting_deadline`). Signing or submitting a transaction before the deadline does not reserve a Vote; a transaction that lands after the deadline is rejected.

**Competing submissions.** The first accepted Vote transaction for a wallet-and-round pair creates the wallet's immutable position. Any retry or competing transaction that reaches the program afterward is rejected without changing the recorded outcome or stake and without locking additional USDC. A rejected or otherwise failed Vote transaction creates no position and incurs no Opal Voting Fee, although external network or provider execution costs may still apply. A client that receives an already-voted error must read the existing position rather than assume no Vote exists.

**Vote-stage payout boundary.** All fees, refunds, slashing, and pro-rata rewards are calculated from the co-located Vote Settlement Vault. Every eligible participant—including the asserter and either disputer—must claim their vote-stage payout into a Private Voting Balance after the Vote Round finalizes. During Vote Initialization, Opal creates and sponsors any missing private balance for the three bonded participants so recipient setup cannot block voting or a later claim. Sponsorship adds no participant fee and does not grant Opal token authority; only the wallet can authenticate to view or withdraw its balance. Non-vote settlement paths continue paying public Solana token accounts automatically. A manual withdrawal is required to move a claimed private balance back to Solana. See [ADR-0009](0009-claim-based-vote-payouts.md).

**MVP privacy boundary.** During the Voting Window, the Private Voting Balance, per-outcome totals, wallet choices, and stake amounts are permissioned/private. Public Solana still exposes deposits into and withdrawals from the private balance. After settlement, the final outcome, whether the 67% threshold was reached, aggregate stake per outcome, the wallet-to-outcome-and-stake mapping, and each position's exact payout amount and `Claimable`, `Claimed`, or `Slashed` status are public. A wallet's total Private Voting Balance remains private. Permanent voter privacy is a possible post-MVP improvement, not an MVP guarantee.

**Considered options.** Public + anti-sniping (rejected: bandwagon-prone); on-chain commit-reveal (viable; rejected for MVP in favour of MagicBlock's amount-hiding and no-reveal UX); keeping bonds on Solana during voting (rejected: the unified pool would require multi-step cross-runtime payouts and partial-settlement recovery); quadratic + proof-of-personhood (`Vision`).

**Consequences.** MagicBlock is the MVP's critical-path dependency (private-balance authentication, delegation, router placement, PER availability, private token movement, and terminal settlement). Voters must fund before voting; bonded participants do not have to pre-create a payout destination. Every recipient manually withdraws when they want public-wallet liquidity because vote settlement itself does not initiate a withdrawal. Opal charges no protocol fee on Private Voting Balance deposits or withdrawals; external network or provider execution costs may still apply. Opal absorbs sponsored balance-initialization costs as an operational expense covered by ordinary protocol fees. Deposits and withdrawals expose `Pending`, `Completed`, and `Recovery Needed`; only destination-balance confirmation produces `Completed`, so source-transaction confirmation alone never changes usable balance. The balance, Vote positions, and payout claims remain bound to the original wallet. Opal has no admin recovery or destination override for a lost key; any recovery must come from the wallet's own external mechanism. Only that wallet may close its reusable Private Voting Balance, and only after its balance, active locks, and outstanding claims are all zero. Participants receive anti-bandwagon privacy while a vote is active, but should expect their wallet, choice, and stake to be publicly attributable after settlement. The irreducible residual is a 51%-of-stake attack, mitigated by bond/reward sizing and voter-base growth; proof-of-personhood is the `Vision` hardening. Voter-reward sufficiency at small scale is an open economic question. See [ADR-0002](0002-trusted-llm-resolver.md) (the vote backstops the LLM) and [ADR-0004](0004-single-asset-usdc.md).
