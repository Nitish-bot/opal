# Keep public protocol records permanently readable

Assertion, dispute, resolution, and public payout-status records remain permanently readable after their lifecycle completes. Opal exposes no instruction that closes these records for rent recovery. Empty token vaults and transient private infrastructure may close only after their balances, locks, and payout liabilities are zero.

**Why.** Integrators bind irreversible actions to a specific Assertion ID and must be able to verify its statement, canonical Resolution Spec URI, dispute history, final outcome, and participant settlement later. Allowing a participant or administrator to close those records would turn a stable oracle reference into missing state and break audit history. Keeping the public record available is more important than recovering its account-creation lamports.

**Consequences.** The payer of a permanent public account should treat its account-creation cost as non-recoverable. Resolved Assertions, completed disputes, resolution rounds, and per-voter `VotePositionAccount` records in `Claimable`, `Claimed`, or `Slashed` state cannot be deleted or reused. Vote positions use separate accounts rather than a growable vector on the round; see [ADR-0017](0017-per-voter-public-position-accounts.md). A Vote Settlement Vault cannot close while any payout liability remains, including a non-expiring unclaimed payout. Closable vaults and transient private accounts require explicit zero-balance, zero-lock, and zero-liability checks. Their creation payer is recorded as the immutable rent-refund recipient, and any reclaimed lamports return there: if Opal or an integrator sponsored creation, that sponsor receives the refund rather than the participant or treasury. Anyone may close a protocol-owned vault after its safety checks pass because neither eligibility nor the refund destination is caller-controlled. Closing a reusable Private Voting Balance additionally requires its wallet's signature and verifies that no balance, active lock, or outstanding claim remains. Closing transient infrastructure never deletes the corresponding public history.
