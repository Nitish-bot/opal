# Keep public protocol records permanently readable

Assertion, dispute, resolution, and public payout-status records remain permanently readable after their lifecycle completes. Opal exposes no instruction that closes these records for rent recovery. Empty logical token-balance records and transient private infrastructure may close only after their type-specific balances, locks, counters, and liabilities are zero. eSPL's shared per-mint global vault is infrastructure and is not closed per Opal round.

**Why.** Integrators bind irreversible actions to a specific Assertion ID and must be able to verify its statement, canonical Resolution Spec URI, dispute history, final outcome, and participant settlement later. Allowing a participant or administrator to close those records would turn a stable oracle reference into missing state and break audit history. Keeping the public record available is more important than recovering its account-creation lamports.

**Consequences.** The payer of a permanent public account should treat its account-creation cost as non-recoverable. Resolved Assertions, completed disputes, resolution rounds, and per-voter `VotePositionAccount` records in `Claimable`, `Claimed`, or `Slashed` state cannot be deleted or reused. Vote positions use separate accounts rather than a growable vector on the round; see [ADR-0017](0017-per-voter-public-position-accounts.md). The round-scoped logical Vote Settlement Vault record cannot close while any payout liability remains, including a non-expiring unclaimed payout. Its close instruction independently requires zero logical balance, zero active locks, zero positive-unclaimed-position count, and no unstaged fee or pro-rata-dust liability; a generic settled flag is insufficient. Once fee or dust funds move to `TreasuryStagingBalance`, a pending `TreasuryFeeDelivery` or `TreasuryDustDelivery` blocks closure of the staging balance and that delivery record rather than the emptied settlement record. The two delivery records use separate nonces and one-way statuses; neither is reset for the other tranche. Other transient private accounts use type-specific safety checks rather than pretending every account contains token or liability fields. Their creation payer is recorded as the immutable rent-refund recipient, and any reclaimed lamports return there: if a participant or integrating application paid for creation, that payer receives the refund rather than the caller or treasury. Protocol-owned cleanup is permissionless only if the feasibility spike proves its PDA authority path; neither eligibility nor the refund destination is caller-controlled. Closing a reusable Private Voting Balance additionally requires its wallet's signature and verifies only that account's amount and active locks are zero. Outstanding round claims survive closure and require the owner to recreate the same canonical empty destination before claiming. Closing transient infrastructure never deletes the corresponding public history.
