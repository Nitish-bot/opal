# Single versioned trusted resolver job for the MVP; the Switchboard council is dropped

The first-pass dispute resolution is one versioned off-chain resolver job whose accepted verdict—derived from at most one model inference—is posted on-chain by the dedicated `ProtocolConfig.resolver` signer. The on-chain `submit_llm_resolution` instruction and signer gate are `[Built]`; the off-chain job is `[MVP-target]`. It records the verdict and timing on-chain. Binding prompt, response, evidence, or model hashes on-chain is **not** part of the MVP—it is deferred to the `Vision` trust-minimized/permissionless resolver. The former three-feed Switchboard On-Demand “council,” its dependency, and its feed/hash fields have already been removed.

**MVP resolver job.** When the first dispute is accepted, the service snapshots the published resolver-policy version active for that job. The policy pins the provider, an immutable model revision rather than a mutable alias, decoding and tool parameters, prompt template, schema/extraction code, network limits, and output parser. The job retrieves the exact content-addressed Resolution Spec from Arweave, applies the duplicate-key, schema, cross-field, and URL checks in [ADR-0016](0016-versioned-resolution-spec-json.md), then fetches authoritative sources in declared priority order. Each source request is HTTPS-only, carries no ambient credentials, revalidates redirects and public-network destinations, prevents DNS rebinding, and enforces the versioned policy's request, redirect, decompressed-byte, and content-type bounds.

An initial source request may receive at most two retries for a timeout, temporary DNS failure, HTTP 408, 429, or 5xx response, using bounded backoff. Policy denial, invalid TLS, unsupported content, an oversized response, or any other non-retryable failure classifies that source as unavailable immediately. Exhausted transient retries do the same. The job then applies the source's declared `unavailableBehavior`; it never silently skips, substitutes, or invents precedence. An available source whose admissible contents cannot decide the statement is evidence for `Unresolvable`, not a resolver outage. A retrievable, integrity-verified Resolution Spec that fails the pinned structural or cross-field rules deterministically produces `Unresolvable`: the asserter supplied no applicable valid rubric. Failure to retrieve the committed bytes or verify their content-addressed integrity is instead a resolver-service failure and produces no verdict.

After validation and source processing, the service makes at most one logical model inference request. A structurally invalid but verified spec short-circuits to the deterministic `Unresolvable` result above without spending a model call. Otherwise the prompt includes the exact Statement, validated Resolution Spec, source material and retrieval metadata, the policy version, and an output contract limited to `True`, `False`, or `Unresolvable` plus a non-normative rationale. Retrieved source text is isolated and labeled as untrusted evidence: it cannot add instructions, alter the Statement or spec, or override higher-priority resolver policy. Model tools are disabled for the MVP; every network retrieval happens through the pinned fetcher before inference. A transport retry is allowed only through a provider idempotency key that guarantees it refers to the same logical request; after an ambiguous or completed inference, the service does not make a fresh unkeyed call, switch models, vote among samples, or substitute a heuristic answer. Invalid output or provider failure produces no verdict. If no valid verdict lands before the 24-hour Resolver Deadline, the existing `ResolverUnavailable` path applies.

The service retains a structured off-chain report keyed by Assertion for at least 90 days: policy/provider/model identifiers, retrieval timestamps and statuses, final source URLs after redirects, response-content hashes, the exact prompt, raw response, parsed verdict, and rationale. This report is diagnostic and may be exposed by the Preview API, but it is not protocol evidence and cannot override the on-chain verdict or Resolution Spec because the MVP does not bind its hash on-chain. The official deployment manifest publishes each resolver-policy version and its activation time; a policy upgrade applies only to jobs accepted after activation.

**Why.** The LLM layer does **not** need to be trustless, because the staked vote is the trust backstop ([ADR-0003](0003-private-staked-voting.md)): a wrong or corrupt verdict is challengeable into the private vote. The council was buying trust-minimization at a layer that is already backstopped, at the cost of operating three live LLM feeds. It was never operationally stood up—no live feeds were configured and tests only called the resolver-gated instruction directly—so removing it lost no working service. A single versioned job is the smallest design that defines reproducible behavior without pretending the centralized result is trustless.

**Considered options.** 3-feed council (rejected: operational overhead, unbuilt, redundant with the vote); single Switchboard feed (rejected for MVP: still net-new feed/job work plus a live external dependency); **trusted resolver (chosen)**.

**Consequences.** The MVP first pass is centralized and must be trusted to fetch, extract, prompt, report, and submit honestly—acceptable only because the vote can override it. Limiting a valid-spec job to at most one logical inference prevents hidden retries from turning the resolver into an undocumented ensemble, but increases the chance that a provider failure reaches `ResolverUnavailable`; an invalid verified spec takes the deterministic zero-inference path. Off-chain reports improve debugging but are not cryptographic provenance. Trust-minimized inference (Switchboard, TEE-attested, or otherwise permissionless) and any on-chain provenance commitment remain documented `Vision` upgrades; their future account shape is not settled here.
